## Fact sources (each claim has 2+ sources; see the topic JSON)
- Mars air under 1% of Earth's: NASA Mars facts and the NSSDC Mars fact sheet.
- Perseverance's chute slowed it to ~200 mph (320 km/h): JPL PIA24316 and Digital Trends. That's about 3× a 65 mph highway speed (derived).
- Venus: ~90× Earth's pressure (Venera 13 measured 89 atm, roughly the pressure ~1 km under the ocean): Guinness World Records and NASA. 465 °C (870 °F), hot enough to melt lead: Space.com and NASA. Sulfuric-acid clouds: NASA and ESA.
- Venera 13 (1982) survived 127 minutes, the record: Guinness and Space.com.
- Jupiter has no solid surface: NASA Jupiter facts. The Galileo probe entered on Dec 7 1995 and returned data for 58 minutes: NASA and FlightGlobal (1995: "57 min"). The transmitter failed 61.4 min after entry, so we say "about an hour".
- Titan: ~1.5× Earth's surface pressure, gravity 14% of Earth's (≈1/7), ~−179 °C: Planetary Society and NASA Titan page.
- Huygens' parachute descent took 2 h 27 min (NASA) / 2 h 28 min (ESA), on Jan 14 2005.
- Flying with strapped-on wings "could even be possible": Planetary Society; Univ. of Leicester Physics Special Topics paper (2014, via phys.org). Hedged as "scientists think you might".

## Visual references
- Mars: real-data Mars map (kit.planet). The Mars 2020 parachute is 21.5 m with red/white gores; the aeroshell is a white conical backshell plus a tan 70° heat shield, 4.5 m (JPL PIA24316 illustration).
- Venus from space: featureless pale-yellow cloud deck, matching visible-light images (Mariner 10 / MESSENGER natural color). The surface uses dark basalt slabs under dim orange light, as in the Venera 13 panoramas. The Venera 13 lander is simplified from NSSDC/Lavochkin photos: a spherical pressure vessel on a ring collar with a disc aerobrake.
- Jupiter: real-data Jupiter map. The cloud decks inside are stylized, darker and redder with depth. The Galileo descent module is a squat body with gold foil under a 2.5 m chute.
- Titan: an opaque orange haze globe with a blue-ish detached haze layer at the limb (Cassini natural color). Saturn's rings appear nearly edge-on because Titan orbits in the ring plane. The surface (dark dunes plus a dark methane lake) is under dim orange light, like the Huygens DISR surface image. Huygens is the official NASA 3D model (Cassini-Huygens (A), without Cassini) under a striped chute.
