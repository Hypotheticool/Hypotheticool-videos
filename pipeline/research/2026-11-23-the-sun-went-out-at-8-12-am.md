## Fact sheet (each claim, 2+ sources)
- Sunlight takes about 8 min 20 s to reach Earth: Live Science (2026, "For at least 8 minutes, 20 seconds, no one would know"); NASA Space Place / How It Works ("first eight minutes").
- The Moon goes dark too (it shines by reflected sunlight): Futurism ("Within 2 seconds, the full moon ... would also go dark"); How It Works; Live Science (Cronin, MIT: "The moon ... would go completely dark"). We say "seconds later".
- Gravity's change also travels at light speed; Earth leaves in a straight line: Futurism; LIGO GW170817 (gravitational waves arrive with light, speed of gravity = c); Live Science (Cronin: planets "fly off in the direction of their current travel"); How It Works ("straight trajectory").
- Earth's speed ~18 mi/s (29.8 km/s): NASA Earth fact sheet (29.78 km/s mean orbital velocity); Futurism ("18 miles per second"). NY→LA ~2,450 mi straight line ÷ 18.5 mi/s ≈ 2 min 12 s → "about two minutes".
- Photosynthesis stops; most plants die within weeks; big trees could last decades: Popular Science ("Photosynthesis would halt immediately, and most plants would die in a few weeks"); How It Works; Live Science (Summers).
- Below 0 °F within a week: Popular Science ("Within a week, the average global surface temperature would drop below 0°F"); Caltech, Dave Stevenson ("Within days to a week ... below the freezing point of salty water"); How It Works ("-18 degrees Celsius" within a week).
- 0 °F = home freezer: FDA (keep freezer at 0 °F / −18 °C); USDA FSIS.
- About −100 °F within a year: Popular Science ("In a year, it would dip to –100°"); Caltech, Stevenson ("below 200 degrees absolute ... roughly minus 100 Fahrenheit").
- Colder than an average South Pole winter day: Amundsen-Scott winter (June–Aug) mean ≈ −60 °C / −76 °F (NSF South Pole station pages; Wikipedia climate table from NOAA data); −100 °F = −73 °C.
- Ocean surface freezes; the ice insulates liquid water below: Popular Science ("that ice would insulate the deep water below"); How It Works. (How long is disputed: Caltech says the deepest water freezes after ~1,000 years, others say far longer, so the script gives no duration.)
- Iceland heats about 9 in 10 homes geothermally: Orkustofnun / National Energy Authority (~90% of homes); Popular Science (87%) and Eric Blackman (Rochester): people "could continue harnessing volcanic heat for hundreds of years".
- Fiction: the characters, their kitchen, their lines and the "one week to reach Iceland" plan.
