## Fact sources (each claim ≥2)
- Carrington saw the flare on 1 Sept 1859 at Redhill near London; "two patches of intensely bright and white light", gone about five minutes later: History.com, Space.com, NOAA NESDIS, Wikipedia. (NOAA says around 11:00 AM; the time isn't shown.)
- CME arrived in 17.6 hours; usually CMEs take several days: Space.com, Wikipedia (citing Cliver & Svalgaard).
- Auroras as far south as Cuba and Hawaii: Space.com (Cuba, Honolulu), History.com (Cuba, Jamaica), Wikipedia (Cuba, Hawaii). NOAA says Panama.
- Rocky Mountain gold miners got up and made breakfast thinking it was morning: Wikipedia, NOAA NESDIS ("coffee, bacon and eggs at 1:00 AM").
- Telegraph lines sparked, shocked operators, set paper on fire: Wikipedia, History.com, NASA (2014, "setting fire to some telegraph offices").
- Boston–Portland (Maine) operators unplugged batteries and kept sending on auroral current alone: History.com, Wikipedia (about two hours).
- March 1989 storm blacked out Quebec, 6 million people for 9 hours: Lloyd's 2013 via Insurance Journal, NASA ("The Day the Sun Brought Darkness", 2009). "Weaker" than Carrington: 1989 Dst ≈ −589 nT vs Carrington estimates −850 to −1,750 nT (Wikipedia, Space.com).
- Feb 2022: a minor geomagnetic storm doomed up to 40 of 49 new Starlink satellites: SpaceX update (8 Feb 2022), NASA/Space.com, CNN.
- Large power transformers can take over a year to replace: US DOE "Large Power Transformers and the U.S. Electric Grid" (lead time 5–16 months, up to 20+), Lloyd's 2013 / National Academies 2008.
- Lloyd's 2013: 20–40 million Americans at risk of outages lasting weeks to 1–2 years; US cost $0.6–2.6 trillion: Insurance Journal, Lloyd's report (openphilanthropy.org copy), Wikipedia.
- $2.6 trillion ÷ ~340 million people ≈ $7,600 each → "over seven thousand per American" (Census 2025 population ~341M). 40 million ≈ California's population (~39.4M, US Census 2024).
- July 23, 2012 CME as strong as Carrington's crossed Earth's orbit; one week earlier and Earth would have been in the line of fire; Daniel Baker: "If it had hit, we would still be picking up the pieces": NASA Science (23 July 2014), PBS NewsHour, CBS News.
- Pete Riley's estimate: ~12% chance of a Carrington-class storm in a decade: NASA Science (2014), Riley 2012 (Space Weather journal), Discover.
Removed: "11:18 AM" (not on the sources), "Quebec grid collapsed in 90 seconds" (not confirmed), "billion-ton CME" (no Carrington-specific mass), Lloyd's "16 days" (report says "several weeks").
