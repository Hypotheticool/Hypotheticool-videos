## Fact sheet (each claim, 2+ sources)
- Below 0 °F average within a week: Popular Science ("Within a week, the average global surface temperature would drop below 0°F"); Caltech, Stevenson ("Within days to a week … below the freezing point of salty water"); How It Works (−18 °C within a week). Live Science (Summers): about 20 °C of cooling per day at first.
- No blue sky, stars visible all day: the blue day sky is scattered sunlight (The Conversation, "Curious Kids"; AMNH: the stars "were there when the Sun was up"). With no Sun, stars stay visible: Live Science ("distant stars in the sky would still be visible"); How It Works.
- Iceland's heat comes from magma heating rock and groundwater: Orkustofnun / National Energy Authority district heating page; Government of Iceland energy page.
- Hellisheiði sends hot water to the capital area: ON Power (on.is, "Our power plants": 303 MW electric, 200 MW thermal); ThinkGeoEnergy (Hellisheiði thermal plant for the capital area's district heating).
- Almost 9 in 10 homes heated geothermally: NEA ("About 90% of the energy used for domestic heating … geothermal", 2020); Government of Iceland ("About 85% of all houses"); Popular Science ("87 percent of its homes"). So we say "almost nine in ten".
- Earth's inner heat is thousands of times weaker than sunlight: internal heat flow 44 TW (Scientific American) / 47 ± 2 TW (Davies & Davies 2010, Solid Earth) ≈ 0.09 W/m² over 5.1×10¹⁴ m²; sunlight absorbed ≈ 240 W/m² (NASA Earth's energy budget). The ratio is ≈ 2,700 (our arithmetic). The on-screen squares are drawn with areas to scale.
- Survival refuges (submarines in the deep ocean; nuclear or geothermal habitats; underground): Popular Science ("Humans could live in submarines in the deepest and warmest parts of the ocean … nuclear- or geothermal-powered habitats"); Live Science, Cronin ("survive underground in caves, sustained by geothermal or nuclear energy"); Caltech, Stevenson ("survive a long time by drilling and creating a habitat deep down").
- Alvin found vent life in 1977 that doesn't need sunlight: WHOI 1977 discovery page (Feb 17, 1977, Galápagos Rift: a food chain that "does not require … sunlight and photosynthesis"); University of Hawaiʻi Exploring Our Fluid Earth ("Rather than conducting photosynthesis, these bacteria conduct chemosynthesis"); Live Science (vent microbes would likely survive).
- Vent water about 400 °C: WHOI ("400° Celsius (750° Fahrenheit)"); NOAA Ocean Service ("over 700° Fahrenheit (340° Celsius)"). Lead melts at 327.5 °C (Royal Society of Chemistry periodic table; Los Alamos periodic table). Even NOAA's 340 °C is above lead's melting point.
- Big trees could survive decades: Popular Science ("Large trees … could survive for several decades, thanks to slow metabolism and substantial sugar stores"); How It Works ("the larger trees would still survive for a few decades").
- Final temperature after millions of years, about −400 °F: Popular Science ("Millions of years after that, our planet would reach a stable –400°"); Caltech, Stevenson (about 30 K, after millions of years); UChicago (about 35 K, core heat); How It Works (−240 °C). Pluto's surface: NASA (about −375 to −400 °F); Live Science.
- The air freezes out: Caltech, Stevenson ("atmosphere was freezing out as solid oxygen and nitrogen"); How It Works ("our atmosphere will freeze as well and collapse to the surface"). No timescale is given, so the script attaches it only to "millions of years".
- Volcanic heat for hundreds of years: Popular Science, Eric Blackman (Rochester): people "could continue harnessing volcanic heat for hundreds of years"; Caltech ("survive a long time"); Live Science (Cronin).
- Fiction: the cast, the house, their lines and the trip to Iceland.
- Cut as unverifiable or disputed: ocean freeze timelines (sources range from 1,000 years to hundreds of thousands); "weather stops" (no direct source); a Milky Way at noon (inference only).
