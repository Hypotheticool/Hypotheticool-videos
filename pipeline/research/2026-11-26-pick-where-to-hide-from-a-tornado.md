## Why this topic
Carried-over runner-up with a clear life-or-death verdict from official sources (NWS, SPC, CDC). The instinct option (the concrete overpass "bunker") is the deadly one, which creates the question in the first second. Real event tie-in: the May 3, 1999 Bridge Creek–Moore tornado (people died at overpasses; radar measured ~300 mph near Bridge Creek). Big-number comparison: ~300 mph is almost double the 157 mph that makes a Category 5 hurricane.

## Fact sources (each claim has 2+ sources)
- Overpass: narrow passage can increase wind speed; higher wind and more flying debris; "safety ... merely an illusion": NWS Norman (weather.gov/oun/safety-overpass: "the narrow passage underneath an overpass might cause an increase in the wind speed under the bridge"); NWS "Severe Weather Facts & Myths" PDF ("Overpasses can concentrate the tornado winds"); FOX Weather (Mar 12 2026); Missouri StormAware ("can act as a wind tunnel"). Script hedges with "can".
- People died at overpasses in Oklahoma on May 3, 1999: NWS Norman ("There were 3 people killed near or underneath highway overpass bridges on May 3"); SPC I-35 & Shields page (one killed "while attempting to take refuge"); SPC I-44 & 16th St. page (one killed, "blown out from under the bridge"). Script gives no number.
- Never try to outrun a tornado in a car: NWS myths PDF ("Never try to outrun a tornado in a vehicle"); CDC ("Don't try to outrun a tornado"). Cars get tossed: SPC via Live Science ("Vehicles are notorious as death traps in tornadoes, because they are easily tossed and destroyed"); SPC FAQ ("Vehicles are extremely risky in a tornado"); NWS Norman 1999 photos (car "airborne and landed upside down", minivan "rolled several times").
- Caught in a car: get out and lie flat in a ditch, cover your head: SPC FAQ ("If you can safely get noticeably lower than the level of the roadway, leave your car and lie in that area"); Missouri StormAware ("get out, and lay flat in a nearby ditch covering your neck and head"); FOX Weather.
- Mobile homes: SPC FAQ ("Most tornadoes can destroy even tied-down mobile homes"); CDC ("Even mobile homes with a tie-down system cannot withstand the force of tornado winds").
- 15 to 20 times more likely to be killed than in a permanent home: NWS Mobile Home Safety one-pager (weather.gov/media/wrn/Mobile-Home-Safety-One-Pager-Final.pdf); NWS Jackson MS (weather.gov/jan/swpw_mhsafety).
- Basement, under sturdy protection, away from windows: SPC FAQ ("Get in the basement and under some kind of sturdy protection (heavy table or work bench)"); CDC ("The safest place in the home is the interior part of a basement"); Live Science/SPC ("away from any windows, under a sturdy workbench or mattress").
- No basement: small inside room on the lowest floor (bathroom/closet): SPC FAQ ("Go to the lowest floor, small center room (like a bathroom or closet)"); CDC ("inside room, without windows, on the lowest floor ... center hallway, bathroom, or closet"); Missouri StormAware.
- As many walls as possible between you and the outside: FOX Weather ("Try to put as many walls—without windows — between you and the outside as possible"); NWS Norman ("get as many walls between you and the tornado as possible"); AccuWeather.
- Flying debris is the greatest danger; cover your head: SPC FAQ ("Flying debris is the greatest danger in tornadoes"; head protection boosts survivability); NWS Norman (debris "responsible for most tornado casualties"); FOX Weather ("protect your head from flying debris"). CDC notes there's no research on helmets, so the script says "cover your head", not "wear a helmet".
- ~300 mph radar-measured winds near Bridge Creek, OK, May 3, 1999: Guinness World Records ("486 +/-32 km/h (302 +/- 20 mph)", estimated, ~100–200 ft above the ground); NWS Norman 1999 event page (radar measurement, "likely will remain above 300 mph"); CBS News ("A 300 mph monster"). On screen: "~300 mph" + "radar estimate".
- Category 5 hurricane = 157 mph or higher sustained wind: NHC Saffir-Simpson scale page; NWS Miami Saffir-Simpson page. 300 ÷ 157 ≈ 1.9 → "almost double". On screen labeled "Category 5 hurricane (minimum)".
- Cut this run (single source or off-length): opening windows myth (cut for length), mobile-home 54%/6% figures (one source), helmet advice (CDC says unproven).
