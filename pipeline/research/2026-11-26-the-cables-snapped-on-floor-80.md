## Fact sheet (each claim ≥2 sources)
- A last-second jump helps only a little. From a 165 ft fall, impact speed drops from 72 to 65 mph, using a ~7 mph jump (Baird, WTAMU). Impact speed = fall speed minus jump speed (The Conversation).
- Governor: flyweights spring outward when the car exceeds its rated speed. The governor rope then pulls the safeties, which clamp onto the guide rails (Otis high-rise safety systems; HowStuffWorks elevator p.5).
- Many cables, far more than needed (Otis). Baird: if all the cables snapped (highly unlikely), the car would fall only a few feet before the safeties engaged.
- Otis, 1854, New York (Crystal Palace exhibition): he had the hoisting rope cut while standing on the platform, and it stopped (Scientific American archive; ASME; American Heritage "All safe, gentlemen, all safe"; PBS). Otis gives 1852 for inventing the brake and 1854 for the demonstration.
- July 28, 1945, 9:40 AM: in thick fog, a B-25 hit the Empire State Building between floors 78 and 80 (Wikipedia; Guinness).
- Betty Lou Oliver, 20, elevator operator, on the 80th floor, was injured. She was put in another car to go down. Its damaged cables snapped and it fell 75 stories, over 1,000 ft (Guinness; Wikipedia; AOL/People).
- Why she likely survived: air pressure in the shaft slowed the car, and the snapped cables coiled up below like a spring (Guinness; Wikipedia credits the air cushion; The Conversation covers coiling cable).
- Injuries: broken neck, back and pelvis (Guinness; Wikipedia). Guinness record: longest fall survived in a lift (Guinness record page; Guinness news).
- On screen only: 3 Statues of Liberty (305 ft ground to torch each, NPS) = 915 ft, still less than the 1,000+ ft fall. 65 mph ≈ US highway speed (IIHS speed-limit table).
