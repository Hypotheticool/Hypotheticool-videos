## Facts and sources (each line has 2+)
- Most avalanches start on 30–45° slopes: Avalanche Canada glossary; REI (Reducing Risk); avalanche.org (slabs 30–50°); Wikipedia.
- Slab breaks loose along a crown fracture: Wikipedia; Avalanche Canada (slab fracture); Utah Avalanche Center.
- Within seconds, up to ~80 mph: Utah Avalanche Center FAQ (dry slabs 60–80 mph within ~5 s); REI (up to 80 mph); National Geographic (up to 100 mph, "as fast as cars on a freeway").
- 9 in 10 fatal avalanches triggered by the victim or their party: Utah Avalanche Center FAQ; National Geographic ("Humans trigger 90 percent of avalanche disasters").
- If caught: get off the slab/to the side, grab a tree, swim; as it slows, clear an air space in front of the mouth, push a hand up: Utah Avalanche Center; National Geographic; Sierra Avalanche Center (exposed body part speeds the search).
- Debris sets up like concrete: Utah Avalanche Center; National Geographic.
- Don't go for help; companion rescue is often the only way: Utah ("Should you go for help? NO!"); avalanche.org Companion Rescue; Eurac Research 2024.
- Asphyxia is the main killer; most have <15 min: avalanche.org; SLF (Switzerland); CMAJ 2011 (Haegeli et al.: 81% asphyxia in Canada).
- Rescue teams far slower (Swiss data: organized ~90 min vs companions ~10 min): Eurac Research 2024 (JAMA Netw Open); avalanche.org; Utah.
- Beacons transmit by default; rescuers switch to search: REI transceiver guide; Sierra Avalanche Center; avalanche.org.
- Probe to pinpoint, then dig; shoveling is the most demanding, time-consuming phase: avalanche.org (Probing; Strategic Shoveling); Sierra Avalanche Center.
- Survival: >90% in the first ~10–18 min (Swiss), drop to 34% by 35 min: CMAJ 2011; Eurac 2024 (high-survival window now ~10 min); Utah (93% within 15 min, 20–30% after 45 min).
- On-screen comparison: ~80 mph ≈ a car on the highway (National Geographic; Utah).
