## Why this one

## Facts and sources (each line has 2+)
- About 1 minute: car usually floats ~1 min (flotation phase 15–63 s) before water reaches the windows. NPR (Giesbrecht/Dworkin), KUOW (Giesbrecht, 30–60 s), GMA, Jalopnik (Kids and Car Safety), JEMS, Reader's Digest.
- Door pinned by hundreds of pounds of force: GMA (Giesbrecht: up to 500 lb with water 1–2 ft up the door), Reader's Digest ("hundreds of pounds"), McDonald & Giesbrecht 2013 review (Dutch trials 750–1000 N ≈ 170–225 lbf, doors could not be opened), NPR. On screen comparison: ≈ a grown man leaning on it, or more (average US adult man ≈ 200 lb, CDC FastStats).
- Don't use the phone until out: KUOW, GMA (Giesbrecht), 2013 review abstract, Reader's Digest, JEMS.
- Seatbelts, Windows, Out (children first): NPR, CBS/KPIX, GMA, JEMS, Injury Epidemiology 2019 (PMC6545703).
- Power windows usually work ~30–60 s: JEMS (Giesbrecht), Reader's Digest (Bassett), CBS ("should work if opened quickly").
- Break a side window near a corner: Reader's Digest (lower corner/edge), PMC6545703 (best strike point = lower corner; 79% wrongly chose the center), CBS.
- Windshield laminated, nearly impossible to break; side windows usually tempered and shatter: Reader's Digest, 2013 review; NPR and GMA note many newer cars use laminated glass (hence "usually").
- Nose (engine end) sinks first; last air at the back: 2013 review, IFLScience, Jalopnik, CBS.
- Help can't arrive in time: Reader's Digest, 2013 review (first minute), NPR.
- ~400 deaths/yr in North America; up to ~10% of drownings: NPR, CBS, JEMS, 2013 review (3–11%), GMA (AAA 2019: 429).
- Old advice (wait to fill and equalize) was wrong; almost no air left by then: JEMS (Giesbrecht: "the wrong thing was taught"), 2013 review abstract, NPR, IFLScience, Jalopnik.
- Kids out first, oldest to youngest: NPR, CBS, KUOW, JEMS, Jalopnik.
