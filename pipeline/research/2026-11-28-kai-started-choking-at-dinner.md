## Facts and sources (2+ each)
- Total blockage = can't speak, cry or cough: Red Cross adult/child choking ("If the airway is totally blocked, the person will not be able to speak, cry or cough"); Mayo Clinic first aid ("Inability to talk").
- Hands at the throat = the choking sign: Mayo ("One or both hands clutched to the throat"); Red Cross checklist ("Holding throat with hand(s)").
- If they can cough forcefully, let them cough: Mayo ("let the person keep coughing… Coughing might naturally remove the stuck object"); Red Cross (encourage coughing).
- Thousands of US deaths a year: NDSC citing NSC Injury Facts ("5,923 people died from choking in 2024"; 4th leading cause of unintentional injury death); UBC "Be Smart, Don't Choke" citing NSC (4,500 in 2009). On screen: 5,923 in 2024; ≈1 every 90 minutes (525,600 min ÷ 5,923 = 89 min).
- Brain damage in ~4 minutes without oxygen: MedlinePlus Choking ("more than four minutes, you could have brain damage or die"); MedlinePlus self-Heimlich ("as little as 4 to 6 minutes"); Cleveland Clinic cerebral hypoxia ("begins within four minutes").
- 5 back blows (heel of hand, between the shoulder blades), then 5 abdominal thrusts, alternate: Red Cross; Mayo ("Alternate between five blows and five thrusts").
- Lips/skin turn blue: Mayo ("Skin, lips and nails that change color turning blue or gray"); Red Cross ("pale or bluish").
- Fist just above the navel, below the ribs, other hand grasps it, pull in and up: Mayo; MedlinePlus ("below your rib cage and above your navel"); Red Cross ("Pull inward and upward").
- How it works (diaphragm up, air forced out of the lungs): AP via STAT ("lift the diaphragm and force air from the lungs to dislodge any object"); Mayo (thrusts). "Like a cork from a bottle" is our analogy for the air-pressure push.
- If they pass out: lower to the floor, CPR, call 911: Red Cross; Mayo.
- Alone: fist above the belly button and thrust over a hard edge (chair, countertop): Mayo ("A countertop or chair will do"); MedlinePlus ("lean over a table edge, chair, or railing").
- Heimlich introduced the maneuver in 1974: NBC, NPR.
- May 2016, age 96, he used it on Patty Ris, 87, choking in the Deupree House dining room (his senior residence) in Cincinnati; "It worked right away": NBC, NPR, AP/STAT. We do NOT say "first time" (NPR notes the BBC says he told them in 2003 he had used it before; NBC added a similar correction).
- Fiction: the cast, the dinner, the dialogue and Kai's rescue.

Cut as unverifiable / disputed: "first time he ever used it", the food she choked on (hamburger vs meat with a bone, sources differ), "choking is the 4th leading cause" (only one source fetched in full).
