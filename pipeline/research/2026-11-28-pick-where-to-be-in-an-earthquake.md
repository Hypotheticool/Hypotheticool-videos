## Why this topic
It is a choice every viewer might face, and three of the four options are things people really do (doorway, run outside, "Triangle of Life"). Each has a clear, checkable verdict from official sources (CDC, USGS, ShakeOut/Earthquake Country Alliance). The winner (Drop, Cover, Hold On under a sturdy table) is useful, and there are two extra beats viewers rarely know (no table → inside wall; in bed → stay and use a pillow). Hook: "Only one of these is the right move." The last line, "So the next time the ground starts to shake, remember:", loops into it.

## Fact sources (each claim has 2+ sources)
- Doorway is a myth; in modern houses doorways are no stronger than any other part of the house and don't protect you from falling or flying objects: CDC "Stay safe during an earthquake" ("In modern houses, doorways are no stronger than any other part of the house"); ShakeOut Drop/Cover/Hold On page ("In modern houses and buildings, doorways are no safer, and they do not protect you from flying or falling objects"); Cal OES "Earthquake myths" (2023); ABC7 (2024, Derek Lambeth, California Earthquake Early Warning Program).
- The myth's origin is an early earthquake photo of a collapsed adobe home with only the door frame standing: ShakeOut ("An early earthquake photo is a collapsed adobe home with the door frame as the only standing part"); Clackamas County ShakeOut ("only true for old, unreinforced adobe houses or some older woodframe houses"); Live Science ("From this came the belief that a doorway is the safest place to be").
- Falling and flying objects cause most injuries: CDC ("Most earthquake-related injuries and deaths are caused by falling or flying objects"; doorways don't protect from "the most likely source of injury − falling or flying objects"); ShakeOut ("much more likely to be injured by falling or flying objects (TVs, lamps, glass, bookcases, etc.) than to die in a collapsed building").
- Don't run: ShakeOut ("Trying to run in an earthquake is dangerous, as the ground is moving"); CDC ("DO NOT run outside or to other rooms during an earthquake"); USGS FAQ ("DON'T run downstairs or rush outside while the building is shaking"); Clackamas ("Trying to move during shaking puts you at risk").
- Outside, glass, bricks and building parts may be falling: ShakeOut ("Running outside is especially dangerous, as glass, bricks, or other building components may be falling"); CDC ("Windows, facades, and architectural details are often the first parts of the building to collapse").
- Triangle of Life: a circulating email; leading experts say its source is discredited: ShakeOut ("an e-mail has circulated…"; "the source has been discredited by leading experts"); Clackamas ("the credibility of these recommendations has been broadly questioned"; it "erroneously advises to get next to a table rather than underneath it"); Snopes (rated MIXTURE, quotes the American Red Cross: "Drop, Cover, and Hold On!" has saved lives in the US). The on-screen email is a paraphrase, labeled as such, not a quote.
- Drop, Cover, Hold On under a sturdy table; hold on with one hand, move with it if it shifts: ShakeOut ("DROP where you are, onto your hands and knees"; "If a sturdy table or desk is nearby, crawl underneath for shelter"; "hold on to it with one hand; be ready to move with your shelter if it shifts"); CDC ("underneath a sturdy table or desk"); USGS ("Get under a desk or table and hang on to it").
- No table: next to an interior wall, cover head and neck: ShakeOut PDF (Earthquake Country Alliance: "crawl next to an interior wall (away from windows)"); CDC ("get down near an interior wall").
- In bed: stay there, protect your head with a pillow: CDC ("If you are in bed, hold on and stay there, protecting your head with a pillow"); ShakeOut PDF ("Do not get out of bed"; cover head and neck with a pillow); Live Science.
- Millions practice every October (Great ShakeOut, third Thursday of October): FEMA 2022 event page ("annual … drill held on the third Thursday of October"; "millions across the world"); USGS 2019 ShakeOut story ("join millions of people"); CBS News 2023 ("At least 56 million people are registered to participate internationally" — not used on screen, single source).
- Not used: injury percentages (no official percentage found in two sources), Doug Copp's personal claims, 1933 Long Beach (not verified in two sources this run).
