## Facts and sources (each line has 2+ sources; see the topic json for URLs)
- Blocked tailpipe → exhaust/CO builds up inside the car, fast: CDC PSA; Griffin et al., BMC Emergency Medicine 2004 (lethal levels within minutes); ABC7 NY Maplewood FD test; CDC 1996 blizzard release.
- CO colorless and odorless: CDC PSA; CDC 1996; PBS/AP 2026.
- Alarm in under 90 seconds: ABC7 NY (Jan 30, 2016: "After only 1 minute and 24 seconds… 90 parts per million"); Snopes repeats the test. On screen 1:24 and 90 PPM.
- Symptoms headache, dizziness, confusion, drowsiness → unconsciousness; "flu-like": CDC CO basics; PBS/AP; CDC 1996.
- Over 200× affinity for hemoglobin: ATSDR Toxicological Profile ("over 200 times greater"); UKHSA ("245-fold"). Relatable comparison on screen: it takes the seats oxygen needs (CO binds the same hemoglobin sites).
- Blood carries less oxygen to the organs: PBS/AP; ATSDR.
- Sleeping people can die before symptoms: CDC CO basics; ABC7 (fire chief: you might go unconscious before you can act).
- Stranded: stay with the car, run the motor about 10 minutes each hour, clear snow from the exhaust pipe, open a window a little: NWS winter safety one-pager; Mass.gov; Maine MEMA (NSC). Michigan State Police says 5–10 minutes (not used verbatim; fetch was partial).
- Window alone not enough: BMC 2004 (windows open 1 inch still lethal within 5 min; only a fully cleared tailpipe was safe); CDC MMWR 1982 ("ventilation adequacy is hard to determine").
- Blizzard of '78 (February 1978): ~3,500 vehicles stranded on Route 128 near Boston: NWS Boston "Blizzard Bits" (3,500 cars); PBS/AP (about 3,000 cars and 500 trucks).
- Deaths from CO in snowbound cars: CDC MMWR 1982 (eastern MA: 8 people stranded in cars died, 5 from CO); New England Historical Society and PBS/AP (14 died of CO in snowbound vehicles). The counts differ by scope, so the script gives no number ("some… died").
- Cut as unverifiable with two sources this run: annual US CO death count (CDC only: 400+ non-fire), the 12-inch clearance rule (BMC only), the 2026 Newport case (AP only).
