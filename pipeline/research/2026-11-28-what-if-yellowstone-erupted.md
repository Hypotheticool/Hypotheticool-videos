## Facts and sources (each claim ≥2 reputable sources)
- Biggest eruption (Huckleberry Ridge, ~2.1 Ma) 2,450 km³ ≈ **2,500× Mount St. Helens 1980** (erupted material): USGS eruption-history table (usgs.gov/volcanoes/yellowstone/summary-eruption-history); USGS YVO "Pantheon of caldera eruptions" (usgs.gov/observatories/yvo/news/pantheon-caldera-eruptions-where-does-yellowstone-rank); NPS Old Faithful VC exhibit (nps.gov/features/yell/ofvec/exhibits/eruption/volcanoes/compare.htm). (USGS FS 2005-3024 and NPS also give "6,000×" for magma volume; we use the erupted-material framing only.)
- **Texas 11 ft:** our arithmetic: 2,450 km³ ÷ Texas area 695,662 km² (US Census) = 3.5 m = 11.6 ft.
- Caldera **~30 × 45 miles** (NPS; USGS FS 2005-3024 says 35 × 50 mi; USGS table 85 × 45 km) → "the size of Rhode Island" (RI 1,214 sq mi total / 1,034 land; a 30×45-mi ellipse ≈ 1,060 sq mi). On screen we draw USGS's 35 × 50 mi (55 × 80 km) ellipse, labeled approximate, next to Rhode Island at the same scale (US Census outline via us-atlas); at 30 × 45 mi the caldera looked clearly smaller than RI's drawn outline, so the larger USGS figure keeps the comparison honest.
- Pyroclastic flows ("glowing avalanches of rock and gas") at **700–850 °C**, reached >100 km (USGS YVO "How long does a big Yellowstone explosive eruption last?"); "hot flows of rock and ash" devastate the area around (USGS "No, Yellowstone isn't going to wipe out humanity"; USGS FS 2005-3024; The Conversation/UCL Sept 2026). Aluminum melts at 660 °C (standard value; CRC Handbook / Britannica).
- Ash cloud spreads **in all directions, even against the wind**, >1,500 km upwind; **Billings >1 m (>3 ft)**, **Midwest centimeters (a few inches)**, **NYC/LA/Miami millimeters**: Mastin, Van Eaton & Lowenstern 2014, G-cubed — AGU press release (news.agu.org/press-release/yellowstone-supereruption-would-send-ash-across-north-america), USGS abstract (pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70193627), USGS summary page (usgs.gov/volcanoes/yellowstone/modeling-ash-distribution-yellowstone-supereruption-2014), Science News. Map labeled "simplified from USGS ash model" (bull's-eye rings at 300/900/1,800 km are illustrative, not isopachs).
- Kitchen counter = 36 in (standard US counter height; NKBA guideline / common building references).
- Ash = **hard, jagged rock and natural glass**, not soft: USGS FS 027-00 (pubs.usgs.gov/fs/fs027-00), USGS volcanic ash page (volcanoes.usgs.gov/volcanic_ash/ash.html).
- **~4 inches can cave in weak roofs**; wet ash up to 2× heavier: USGS roof-loading page (volcanoes.usgs.gov/volcanic_ash/roof_loading.html), USGS FS 027-00.
- **Melts inside jet engines and makes them stall:** USGS ash-and-aircraft page (volcanoes.usgs.gov/volcanic_ash/ash_clouds_air_routes_effects_on_aircraft.html); Smithsonian Air & Space (May 2025).
- Sulfur aerosols dim sunlight and cool the planet for years: USGS "climate change as a result of a large eruption" page; USGS supervolcano Q&A ("years to decades").
- **Probably up to ~1.5 °C:** NASA GISS / Columbia, McGraw et al. 2024 (Journal of Climate) — nasa.gov/general/can-volcanic-super-eruptions-lead-to-major-cooling-study-suggests-no; news.columbia.edu. **Pinatubo 1991 ≈ 0.7 °C** (USGS FAQ "Could a large Yellowstone eruption significantly change climate?"; NASA says ~0.5 °C) → "about twice".
- **Not overdue:** USGS FAQ (usgs.gov/faqs/yellowstone-overdue-eruption-when-will-yellowstone-erupt); USGS future-activity page.
- **~1 in 730,000 per year ≈ a 1-km asteroid hitting Earth:** USGS "Questions about supervolcanoes" (usgs.gov/volcanoes/yellowstone/questions-about-supervolcanoes); Discover Magazine; TIME quoting USGS's Jacob Lowenstern.
- **Melt 16–20% ("about a fifth") vs ~35–50% likely needed ("at least a third")**, reservoir ~5 km down: Maguire et al. 2022, Science — USGS YVO (usgs.gov/observatories/yvo/news/yellowstones-magma-reservoir-comes-sharper-focus), Science News, Smithsonian.
- **Most likely next = hydrothermal (steam) explosion or a lava flow:** USGS FAQ (usgs.gov/faqs/what-type-eruption-will-yellowstone-have-if-it-erupts-again); USGS future-activity page; USGS FS 2005-3024.
- Cut as unverifiable / single-source: population numbers and death tolls (The Conversation's are from a fictional thought experiment); an exact Tambora cooling number.
