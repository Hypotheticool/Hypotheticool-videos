## Facts and sources (each line has 2+)
- 17 h awake ≈ 0.05% BAC; 24 h ≈ 0.10%, above the U.S. 0.08 limit — CDC/NIOSH "Impaired" (archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/niosh/emres/longhourstraining/impaired.html); OSHA "Drowsy driving" (osha.gov/motor-vehicle-safety/drowsy-driving: same 17 h ≈ .05, 24 h ≈ .10); Sleep Foundation "Drowsy driving vs drunk driving" (~18 h ≈ 0.05%, 24 h ≈ 0.1%).
- Microsleeps: brief sleep episodes (≤15 s per Sleep Foundation; ≤30 s per Cleveland Clinic), people often don't realize — sleepfoundation.org/sleep-deprivation/microsleep; health.clevelandclinic.org/what-you-should-know-about-microsleep. (Script says "seconds at a time", no exact number, because the two disagree.)
- By the third day without sleep, hallucinations are common (visual in 90% of studies) — Waters et al. 2018, Frontiers in Psychiatry, systematic review of 21 studies, 760 people (pubmed 30042701); NPR 2017 "The haunting effects of going days without sleep". Script hedges: "many people".
- Randy Gardner, 17, San Diego, Dec 28 1963 – Jan 8 1964, 11 days (264 h); moody, memory/concentration problems, paranoia; slept 14 h (NPR) / 14 h 46 min (Wikipedia) then recovered; follow-ups found no significant differences — NPR 2024 (npr.org/2024/01/28/1227217274); Wikipedia "Randy Gardner sleep deprivation experiment"; KPBS/GPB reprints.
- Guinness stopped tracking/accepting sleep-deprivation records for safety (1996 per NPR, 1997 per Wikipedia; no year shown on screen) — NPR 2024; Wikipedia.
- Adult body about 60% water (men ~60%, women ~55%) — USGS Water Science School "The Water in You"; Jéquier & Constant 2010, Eur J Clin Nutr.
- Sweat in heat 1–1.5 L per hour — Scientific American (Packer, "How long can the average person survive without water?"); Live Science 32320. 3 × 12-oz cans = 1.06 L.
- Dehydration: blood volume drops, fast pulse, cells (incl. brain) shrink, kidneys usually fail first — Live Science 32320; Scientific American (Packer); Discover 2021 ("kidneys eventually shut down").
- ~10% body-weight water loss = severe/dangerous; 11–12% "incompatible with life" — Live Science (NHS 2009 guidance); Discover 2021. 10% of a ~150–190 lb adult = 15–19 lb ≈ 1.8–2.3 gal → "about two gallons".
- Survival without water: ~2 days to a week (Live Science); a week or more in comfort, hours in extreme heat (Scientific American); ~10 days lying in an air-conditioned room (Discover). Script: "In a cool room, you might last a week. In extreme heat, only hours."
- Verdict: sleep loss for 3 days is miserable and impairing but recoverable (Gardner recovered after sleep; Waters review), 3 days without water can already be life-threatening (all three water sources). Option A wins.
