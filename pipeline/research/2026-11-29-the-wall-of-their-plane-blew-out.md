## Facts and sources (each with 2+ sources)
Characters, the flight and the dialogue are fiction; the physics, the advice and the Alaska 1282 twist are real.
- Sunday after Thanksgiving = busiest day to fly: Time Out per TSA (https://www.timeout.com/usa/news/this-is-the-worst-day-to-travel-for-thanksgiving-weekend-according-to-the-tsa-111826); 2025 record 3.13M on that Sunday (https://www.pbs.org/newshour/amp/nation/tsa-screened-more-than-3-million-travelers-at-u-s-airports-sunday-setting-a-record, https://www.hstoday.us/subject-matter-areas/transportation/sunday-after-thanksgiving-was-the-busiest-travel-day-in-tsa-history/).
- Cruise ~35,000 ft; outside about −65 °F: Jalopnik "-66 degrees Fahrenheit" at 35,000 ft (https://jalopnik.com/1910073/plane-loses-cabin-pressure-what-happens/); International Standard Atmosphere ≈ −54 °C (−65 °F) at 35,000 ft (standard lapse rate). A home freezer is 0 °F (FDA), so "way colder than your freezer" is true.
- Fog/mist and a temperature drop in a rapid decompression; loose items fly: Jalopnik; NTSB 1282 release "some passengers' belongings were sucked out of the airplane" (https://www.ntsb.gov/news/press-releases/pages/NR20250624.aspx).
- Time of useful consciousness at 35,000 ft ≈ 30 s to 1 min: FAA AC 61-107B table (https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC_61-107B_CHG_1.pdf), Jalopnik ("as low as 30 to 60 seconds"), PopSci ("30 seconds to a minute", https://www.popsci.com/what-happens-to-your-body-when-plane-loses-pressure/). FAA hypoxia page: after rapid decompression TUC drops to 1/3–1/2 (https://www.faa.gov/pilots/training/airman_education/topics_of_interest/hypoxia).
- Your own mask first, or you may pass out while helping: NZ Herald (Angus Kidman) (https://www.nzherald.co.nz/travel/plane-safety-why-you-should-always-fit-your-own-oxygen-mask-first/6SJPWHNGBMGOCAZXPBZWSKLNM4), PopSci.
- Pull the mask down to start the flow; the bag may not inflate even though oxygen flows: ATSB safety issue AO-2008-053-SI-02 (https://atsb.gov.au/safety-issues/AO-2008-053-SI-02), Yahoo "What happens if your oxygen mask doesn't inflate" (https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/happens-oxygen-mask-doesnt-inflate-120001794.html), Wikipedia Emergency oxygen system.
- Masks carry at least 10 minutes of oxygen; pilots descend to ~10,000 ft where you can breathe: Jalopnik ("at least 10 minutes", "below 10,000 feet"), PopSci ("get the plane down to 10,000 feet or below").
- Alaska Airlines 1282, Jan 5, 2024, ~6 min after takeoff from Portland, plug departed climbing through 14,830 ft (NTSB); media say 16,000 ft (AP via ADN, KPTV), so we say "almost fifteen thousand feet" per the NTSB. Plug covered an unused emergency exit (AP: 2 ft × 4 ft).
- Two seats closest to the opening were unoccupied (AP via ADN https://www.adn.com/alaska-news/aviation/2025/06/24/systemic-failures-led-to-door-plug-flying-off-boeing-737-max-during-alaska-airlines-flight-ntsb-says/); KPTV/Homendy: empty seats next to the hole in row 26. No one was killed; 7 passengers + 1 flight attendant minor injuries (NTSB, AP).
- The four bolts meant to secure the plug were missing (NTSB release; KPTV https://www.kptv.com/2025/06/25/ntsb-blames-boeing-portland-flight-door-plug-break-details-chilling-moments-crew/?outputType=amp; AP). Probable cause: Boeing's inadequate training, guidance and oversight (NTSB).
- An iPhone fell from the flight and was found under a bush by a road, still working, in airplane mode, half battery (CBS https://www.cbsnews.com/news/iphone-sucked-out-alaska-airlines-plane-fell-16000-feet-found-still-works, ADN https://adn.com/nation-world/2024/01/08/heres-how-an-iphone-survived-a-16000-foot-fall-from-a-plane, The Drive). 14,830 ft ≈ 2.8 mi → "almost three miles". NTSB confirmed two phones found.
- Keep your seatbelt fastened whenever seated: FAA "Buckle up" guidance; the 1282 belongings and a passenger's shirt were pulled toward the hole (AP, KPTV).
