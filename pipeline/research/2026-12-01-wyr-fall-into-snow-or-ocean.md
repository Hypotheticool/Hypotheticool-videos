## Fact sources (each claim has at least 2 sources)
- **Terminal velocity ~120 mph, reached in ~13–14 s.** Guinness World Records, "Highest fall survived without parachute": 99% of terminal velocity after 573 m, which takes 13–14 s, at 117–125 mph. NPR (2018, physicist Rhett Allain): "around 120 miles per hour". Script says "about fourteen seconds" and "about 120 mph". The badge adds ~195 km/h.
- **"Almost twice highway speed."** Most US interstate limits are 65–70 mph (IIHS speed-limit table; US DOT/FHWA). 120/65 ≈ 1.85. The bar chart shows 65 mph.
- **Water barely compresses.** USGS Water Science School, "Water compressibility": water is "essentially incompressible". Hitting water from height is "like hitting concrete": askamathematician.com (2012), ScienceABC (belly flops). Script hedges with "almost like concrete".
- **Water tends to knock people out, and they may drown.** NPR 2018, quoting free-fall researcher Jim Hamilton: "water tends to knock people out… even if they survive, they may drown". Interesting Engineering: "landing in water tends to knock people out".
- **Snow is a better landing than water.** NPR (Hamilton: snow "might be a better option than water"). Interesting Engineering: snowbanks have helped people survive.
- **Fresh snow is up to 95% air.** NSIDC, "Why snow matters": fresh, uncompacted snow "typically contains 90 to 95 percent trapped air". UBC ATSC 113: new snow is 30–300 kg/m³, or 5–20% water content.
- **It's the sudden stop (deceleration) that kills.** Interesting Engineering: "it's the deceleration that kills you". NPR (Allain on spreading the stop out). The stopping diagram is labeled "not to scale".
- **Alkemade.** The night of 24/25 March 1944. Rear/tail gunner on Lancaster DS664 of 115 Squadron, over Germany. His parachute was on fire, so he jumped without it. He fell 18,000 ft (5,490 m). Young pines and 18 inches of snow broke his fall, near Oberkirchen/Schmallenberg. He had a twisted knee and "not a single bone had been broken". Sources: Wikipedia (Nicholas Alkemade), RAF Museum blog "The Indestructible Alkemade", Bomber Command Museum of Canada, Smithsonian Air & Space "Parachuteless Freaks". 18,000 ft = 3.4 miles, so "three miles" in the hook. He is shown only as a dark silhouette, since we don't depict the real person.
- **Vulović.** 26 January 1972, JAT Flight 367 (DC-9). She fell 10,160 m (33,333 ft) inside the tail end of the fuselage, which came down in thick snow in a wooded area near Srbská Kamenice, Czechoslovakia. She had a fractured skull, both legs broken, three broken vertebrae and a 27-day coma, and survived. It is the Guinness record "Highest fall survived without parachute". Sources: Guinness record page and GWR news (2022), Wikipedia, NPR. A 2009 claim of a lower breakup altitude exists; GWR says black-box data debunked it.
- **Mount Everest is 29,032 ft (8,848.86 m).** Nepal–China joint survey 2020 (National Geographic, BBC). 33,333 ft is higher.
- **Survival is extremely rare.** NPR (Hamilton: "your chances aren't very good"). Smithsonian lists only a handful of such survivals.
